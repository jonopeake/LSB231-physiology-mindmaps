A regulated variable may fluctuate within a normal range. Negative feedback reduces deviations; it does not require a perfectly constant value.

Negative and positive feedback

Negative feedback

Opposes the initial disturbance.

Reduces the deviation of a regulated variable from its normal range and promotes stability.

Pattern: change → detection → corrective response → smaller change

Positive feedback

Amplifies the initial change.

Drives a process further in the same direction until a terminating event or other mechanism stops it.

Pattern: change → detection → amplifying response → larger change

Positive feedback is not generally a mechanism for maintaining a variable near its normal range; it supports particular processes that need amplification.
