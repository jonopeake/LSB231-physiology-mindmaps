Motor units and force development

Explore how the nervous system controls skeletal muscle force through motor unit recruitment and stimulation frequency.

What is a motor unit?

Choose one motor unit. Each illustration shows one motor neuron. Follow its single axon as it branches into terminals that contact individual muscle fibres at neuromuscular junctions.

Small motor unit

Size principle: smaller, more easily activated motor neurons are generally recruited before larger motor neurons as force demand increases. Larger motor units typically innervate more muscle fibres and contribute more force when recruited.

Recruitment: from low to high force

Move the slider to recruit additional motor units. Each circle represents one motor neuron. Its axon branches to contact individual muscle fibres at neuromuscular junctions (yellow markers). Watch the number of active fibres and muscle tension increase.

Small: 2 fibresMedium: 4 fibresLarge: 6 fibres
Fine versus powerful movements: Muscles that make small, precise movements (such as those controlling the fingers or eyes) often have small motor units, with relatively few muscle fibres per neuron. This allows fine control. Muscles involved in powerful movements (such as those of the thigh) also contain larger motor units, which activate many fibres together and contribute more force. Within either type of muscle, force increases by recruiting additional motor units.
