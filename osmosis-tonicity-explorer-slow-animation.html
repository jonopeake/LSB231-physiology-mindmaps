Concept map: osmosis and tonicity

Plasma membrane

A selectively permeable barrier that allows some substances to pass while preventing others from crossing.

Osmosis

A type of passive transport in which water moves across the plasma membrane in response to differences in solute concentration.

Aquaporins

Special pores formed by membrane proteins that allow water to pass readily across the plasma membrane.

Osmotic pressure

The greater the solute concentration, the greater the osmotic pressure and the tendency for water to move into that solution.

Net water movement

Water moves overall towards the side with the higher concentration of solutes that cannot cross the membrane.

Tonicity

The ability of a solution to change the size and shape of cells by altering the amount of water they contain.

Tonicity depends on solutes that cannot readily cross the membrane. The simulation assumes the illustrated solute remains on its own side.

Compare the three conditions

Isotonic

The solution has the same effective solute concentration as the cell. Water moves in and out at the same rate; the cell maintains its size and shape.

Hypertonic

The solution has a higher effective solute concentration than the cell. Net water movement is out of the cell, causing it to shrink.

Hypotonic

The solution has a lower effective solute concentration than the cell. Net water movement is into the cell, causing it to swell and potentially rupture.
