Hypothalamic–pituitary integration

Explore how the hypothalamus controls hormone secretion through two different pituitary pathways.

Hormonal control: anterior pituitary

The hypothalamus releases regulatory hormones into a specialised portal blood system. These hormones act on anterior pituitary cells, which synthesise and secrete their own hormones.

Neural control: posterior pituitary

ADH and oxytocin are synthesised by hypothalamic neurons. They travel in vesicles along the hypothalamic–neurohypophyseal tract to the posterior pituitary, where action potentials trigger their release into the blood.

Two pathways, one control centre

Both pathways begin in the hypothalamus, but differ in how signals reach the pituitary and where hormones are synthesised.

Anterior pituitary

Blood-borne regulatory signals

  • Hypothalamic releasing or inhibiting hormones enter the portal circulation.
  • They act on anterior pituitary endocrine cells.
  • The anterior pituitary synthesises and secretes its own hormones.
  • Examples: TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, GH and prolactin.
Posterior pituitary

Direct neural connection

  • Hormones are synthesised in hypothalamic neurons.
  • They travel along the hypothalamic–neurohypophyseal tract to the posterior pituitary.
  • Action potentials trigger hormone release into blood.
  • Examples: ADH and oxytocin.

Key distinction: The anterior pituitary is regulated by hormones carried in portal blood; the posterior pituitary releases hormones made in the hypothalamus.
