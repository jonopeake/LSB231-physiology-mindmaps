Compare the transport mechanisms

The descriptions and examples follow the Week 1 teaching material.

Simple diffusion

Passive transport
DirectionHigh → low concentration
Cellular energy (ATP)Not required
PathwayDirectly through the membrane, particularly for small lipid-soluble molecules
Key ideaMovement down a concentration gradient, driven by kinetic energy

Facilitated diffusion

Passive transport
DirectionDown a concentration or electrochemical gradient
Cellular energy (ATP)Not required
PathwayChannel or carrier proteins
ExamplesIons (e.g. sodium, potassium and chloride) through channels; glucose and amino acids through carriers

Active transport

Energy-dependent transport
DirectionLow → high concentration (against the gradient)
Cellular energyRequires cellular energy (ATP)
PathwaySpecialised transport proteins
ExamplesIons, sugars and amino acids
Channels and carriers: Channel proteins provide pathways for particular ions or other substances. Carrier proteins bind their cargo and change shape to move it across the membrane. Both can support facilitated diffusion; specialised carriers can also mediate active transport.
