Negative and positive feedback

Negative feedback

Reverses or counteracts the original change.

Changes in internal conditions trigger a response that restores the system towards its set point.

Pattern: change → receptor → control centre → effector → response that counteracts the change

Positive feedback

Intensifies or amplifies the original change rather than reversing it.

These mechanisms are usually short-lived and have specific functions. Examples include blood clotting and uterine contractions during childbirth.

Pattern: change → amplifying response → greater change

Unlike negative feedback, positive feedback amplifies the initial change rather than restoring the variable towards its set point.
