After release: Sperm travel to the epididymis, where they acquire motility and an increased capacity for fertilisation; they are stored there until ejaculation.

Supporting cells of the testes

Sertoli cells

Within seminiferous tubules. Respond to FSH and support and nourish developing sperm.

Leydig cells

Between seminiferous tubules. Respond to LH by producing testosterone, which supports sperm production.

Spermatogenesis begins at puberty and continues throughout adult life.

Important distinction: Ovulation releases a secondary oocyte. The secondary oocyte completes its final developmental stage only if fertilisation occurs.

Ovarian development

Before birth

Primary oocytes form before birth and remain dormant within ovarian follicles.

From puberty

Follicles develop cyclically. Typically one dominant follicle releases a secondary oocyte at ovulation.

Oogenesis produces a limited number of gametes over the reproductive lifespan.

Hormonal supportFollicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) acts on Sertoli cells; luteinising hormone (LH) stimulates Leydig-cell testosterone productionFollicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) promotes follicle development; the luteinising hormone (LH) surge triggers ovulation

Both processes produce haploid gametes through developmental stages, but their timing, outputs and maturation differ.
