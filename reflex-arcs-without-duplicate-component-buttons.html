How are reflexes organised?

Reflexes are fast, automatic responses to stimuli. Their pathways can differ in the number of synapses and in the type of effector activated.

Monosynaptic reflex

The sensory neuron synapses directly with a motor neuron. The patellar stretch reflex is the example in your teaching material.

Polysynaptic reflex

One or more interneurons connect sensory and motor neurons within the CNS. Most reflexes are polysynaptic.

Classify by the effector

Somatic reflex

Activates skeletal muscle.

Example: the patellar reflex, which causes the quadriceps to contract.

Autonomic reflex

Activates smooth muscle, cardiac muscle or glands.

Like somatic reflexes, autonomic reflexes are automatic responses.

Where does integration occur?

For spinal reflexes, the integration centre is in the spinal cord (grey matter). For cranial reflexes, it is in the brainstem. The integration centre may involve interneurons, but a monosynaptic reflex does not require an interneuron between its sensory and motor neurons.
